ManifestoA perspective on the future of human-computer interaction.Read +

Technology should expand the ways we express ourselves.

Computer interaction is already changing. Speaking to a model instead of typing a prompt is a visible first shift. But the goal is not simply to replace one input method with another. It is to move beyond interfaces that ask people to adapt to the machine.

I dream of a future in which the mind can connect more directly with the machine. Technology should give people more direct ways to turn intention into action, ideas into work, and curiosity into creation, without demanding constant attention, taking away control, or making human interaction feel less human.

I want to help build that future.

The vision

A non-invasive brain-computer interface, small enough to live in a pair of glasses.

Nora's goal is to make interaction between humans and computers effortless. Not a surgical implant, not a research cap of electrodes, and not a headset you put on for a session. It is a frame you would already be wearing. Every interface we use today asks for a detour: reach for a device, unlock it, find the app, type. Nora is about shortening that path until acting on a thought costs almost nothing.

Effortless does not mean the machine guesses what you want. It means the distance between deciding something and the computer doing it gets as short as the technology honestly allows. Subtle physiological expressions are where that starts: small, deliberate traces of intention that a wearable device could plausibly detect without requiring touch or speech.

Approach

Rapid hardware prototyping, careful validation of each sensing method, and research into active sensing beyond passive ExG.

MK1Single-channel proof of captureMay 2026 · 1 × BioAmp EXG Pill · XIAO onboard ADCArchived +

The first prototype answered one question: can a biosignal be picked up at all, digitised, and moved off the device. One analogue amplifier fed the XIAO's built-in ADC, and the samples were streamed over Wi-Fi. Crude, but it proved the capture-and-transport path end to end, and everything after it was measured against that baseline.

MK2Four-channel scaling stageJune 2026 · 4 × BioAmp EXG Pill · XIAO onboard ADCsReference +

MK2 asked whether the approach scaled. Four amplifiers ran into the microcontroller's own converters, which brought electrode contact, ocular and motion artifacts, and timing between channels into view for the first time. It also exposed the ceiling: the onboard ADCs were limited in resolution and calibration, and sensitive to interference from the Wi-Fi radio sitting beside them. Good enough to test whether signals separate, not good enough to build on.

MK3Modular evaluation rigJuly 2026 · 8-channel ADS1299 · 500 SPSIn development +

MK3 replaces the discrete amplifiers and the microcontroller's converters with a dedicated biosignal front end, to find out what a purpose-built chip is actually worth here. It hijacks a TI ADS1299 evaluation board and drives it with a XIAO ESP32-S3, turning a fixed dev kit into a rig I can experiment on: eight channels sampled simultaneously, repeatable raw capture, timing alignment, and a configurable glasses-shaped fixture. Front end, electrode types, and placement can now be tested against each other before any of it is committed to a wearable design.

MK4Dry electrode contact testingAugust 2026 · Brushes & spring-loaded mechanismsTesting +

MK4 moves the contact question into the mechanical design. This setup tests dry electrode concepts in a glasses form factor, including brush contacts and spring-loaded mechanisms. The goal is to compare comfort, contact stability, and repeatability before selecting a direction.

MK4BTwo-camera gaze and context rigSeptember 2026 · 1 eye camera · 1 outward cameraPrototype +

MK4B adds a two-camera layer to the glasses: one inward-facing camera observes a single eye, while one outward-facing camera captures the visible scene. The outside view does not know where the wearer is looking on its own. It becomes useful only when it is aligned with the gaze estimate from the eye camera.

  1. 01 · Eye cameraWhere is the eye directed?Eye features become a gaze estimate.
  2. 02 · Outward cameraWhat is in front of the wearer?The scene provides a shared reference frame.
  3. 03 · FusionWhat could the wearer mean?Time-aligned gaze and scene data narrow the target.

MK4B is camera-only: no biosignals are integrated in this version. The prototype is built to test camera placement, focus, timing, and how an eye-camera gaze estimate can be aligned with the outward-facing scene.

MK4CMultimodal context-based controlSeptember 2026 · Eye tracking · Visual context · EMG confirmationPrototype +

MK4C combines eye tracking, visual context and EMG in one multimodal control loop. The inward-facing camera estimates gaze, the outward-facing camera provides scene context, and the EMG channel serves as deliberate confirmation.

The first integrated demo uses gaze and visual context to select a smart-home device, then turns the intended interaction into a context-based action after an EMG signal. It is an experimental single-scene prototype, not a validated interaction system.

IRInnovation researchOpen sensing directionsOngoing +

Passive ExG is unlikely to be the whole answer. Alongside it, Nora investigates electrode mechanics, inertial sensing for artifact context, and active mechanical and acoustic sensing. These methods probe rather than only listen and may carry information about articulation that surface potentials alone do not. Each is tested on its own before any fusion or product claim.

Nora Lab

The hardware and the experiments need their own software. Nora Lab turns raw streams into traceable sessions without pretending that an unfinished analysis is a result.

LAB 01Exploration dashboardPython · live signal inspectionArchived +

Nora Lab V1 grew out of the first hardware experiments. It was a Python engineering dashboard that could connect to live streams, show raw and filtered traces, inspect spectra, place markers, and export data. It made experimentation tangible, but it also mixed acquisition, analysis, and interface logic too tightly. Timestamps, channel identity, and session provenance were not yet strong enough for reliable multi-channel research.

LAB 02Local experiment platformReact / Next.js · local acquisition serviceIn development +

Nora Lab V2 rebuilt the software around a stricter rule: real data or an honest empty state. The interface was separated from the local acquisition service. The system validates the channel contract, records append-only sessions with manifests, raw samples, and event markers, runs guided protocols, and keeps analysis locked until a versioned pipeline produces a result. The current MK3 Lab carries the same principles into an eight-channel ADS1299 system with live quality views, local USB and UDP connectivity, session replay, and explicit readiness gates.

How I built it

I built Nora Lab with AI-assisted development. Codex and Claude Code helped me implement and review the software, while my own optimized Hermes Agent helped coordinate parts of the workflow. I defined the product direction, experiment logic, system requirements, and validation rules, then used these tools to iterate faster.
